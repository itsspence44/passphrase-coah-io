# Passphrase Coach
 

**1. The Client and the Problem**

Client: Nelsonville Senior Center, Cyber Safety Workshop

Older adults are losing record amounts of money to online fraud, and many are still following outdated password advice. The workshop leader wants a tool participants can use during class to check a passphrase and get friendly, current advice. The rules must follow the current NIST guidance, which favors long passphrases and screening against common passwords over complicated character rules.

---

**2. Why It Matters**

Passwords are important because they help protect people's personal information and online accounts. Weak passwords can make it easier for someone to guess and access an account.

According to the National Institute of Standards and Technology (NIST), password length is an important factor in password strength. NIST also explains that using multiple words in a passphrase can be an effective way to create a longer password.

*Source: NIST – Strength of Passwords*

https://pages.nist.gov/800-63-4/sp800-63b/passwords/ 

Another reason this matters is the increase in online scams. According to AARP's report on FBI and FTC data, Americans reported losing more than $20.9 billion to internet crimes in 2025.

*Source: AARP – FBI Report on Internet Crime Losses*

https://www.aarp.org/money/scams-fraud/fbi-ftc-report-2025-losses.html

This shows why it is important for people to learn how to create stronger passphrases and protect their online accounts.


---

**3. What the Tool Does**

Passphrase Coach is a Python program that helps users check their passphrases.


**The tool will:**

Ask users to enter a passphrase.

Check whether the passphrase meets the minimum length requirement.

Identify weak or common passphrases using its built-in data.

Give users helpful messages when their passphrase needs improvement.

Allow users to continue checking passphrases until they choose to quit.

---

**The tool will not:**

Prevent hackers from accessing company accounts.

Guarantee that a passphrase is completely secure.

Monitor company computers or networks.

Replace other security protections, such as multifactor authentication.
